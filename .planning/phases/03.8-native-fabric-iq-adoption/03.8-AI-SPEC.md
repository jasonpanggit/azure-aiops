# AI-SPEC — Phase 03.8: Native Fabric IQ Adoption

> AI design contract generated by `/gsd-ai-integration-phase`. Consumed by `gsd-planner` and `gsd-eval-auditor`.

## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid — governed multi-agent orchestration with graph traversal and RAG-style Data Agent grounding

**Description:** Native Fabric IQ traversal and curated Gold/Data Agent evidence enrich the existing deterministic operations workflow without replacing local scope enforcement, evidence validation, ledger, budgets, or fallback.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Native results disclose out-of-scope entities or relationships.
2. Fabric IQ or Data Agent output overrides inventory identity or deterministic findings.
3. Evidence is mislabeled, uncited, stale, or attributed to the wrong native/fallback source.
4. Optional native-service failure blocks deterministic operator workflows.
5. Delegated credentials leak into prompts, logs, telemetry, or durable state.

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Enterprise cloud operations — Azure site reliability,
cloud platform engineering, network operations, cloud security, data
governance, and operational audit

**User Population:** SREs and incident commanders, cloud platform and service
owners, network engineers, security analysts, policy/governance owners, Fabric
data and ontology stewards, approvers, and auditors

**Stakes Level:** Critical

**Output Consequence:** Practitioners use these answers to decide which
services and resources may be affected, where to investigate or contain an
incident, whether observed posture violates a control, and whether evidence is
strong enough to support an approval or audit record. Phase 03.8 remains
read-only enrichment and cannot authorize identity, scope, compliance status,
or remediation. Even so, an omitted dependency can delay containment, an
inflated blast radius can cause unnecessary escalation or outage, and a scope
leak can expose sensitive enterprise architecture.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Graph-traversal corruption and blast-radius inflation/omission.** Edge
   direction or predicate is reversed, containment is mistaken for runtime
   dependency, a summarized path changes its meaning, cycles create duplicate
   impact, mixed-case IDs fail to join, or a dangling endpoint disappears.
   High-degree adjacency can inflate impact to an entire subscription, while
   result limits, pagination, stale bindings, or asymmetric filtering can omit
   critical dependents and fabricate a clean or complete state.
2. **Policy-evidence and citation drift across stale generations.** An answer
   uses a superseded policy revision or exception, joins current inventory to
   an older ontology/Gold generation, or retains a citation after its row or
   relationship changed. The link may still resolve while no longer
   supporting the claim, and narrative policy context may be misrepresented as
   a deterministic control result.
3. **Scope leakage through paths, aggregates, and diagnostics.** The seed is
   authorized but a traversed endpoint is not; a relationship, count, owner,
   finding, dropped-edge diagnostic, citation, cache entry, prompt, export, or
   replay then reveals cross-subscription or cross-principal information.
   Filtering only nodes after traversal can also leave inferential leakage in
   totals and path shape.
4. **Deterministic/native disagreement with unavailable fallback.** Native
   Fabric IQ conflicts with inventory or a deterministic check and is silently
   allowed to win, or the OneLake projection is mislabeled as native.
   Disabled, denied, empty, timed-out, malformed, stale, or unavailable native
   services can produce a blank workflow, an unsupported definitive answer,
   repeated retry load, or a fabricated citation instead of an explicit,
   useful deterministic/projection fallback.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No sector-specific law, jurisdictional privacy regime, or formal regulatory
classification has been established for this internal deployment context.
Microsoft Fabric compliance capabilities do not by themselves establish that
a particular regulation applies to this workload; the enterprise privacy,
legal, and compliance owners must determine applicability from deployed
regions, tenant configuration, source data, users, and retention policy.

The directly relevant enterprise constraints are:

- **Authorization and confidentiality:** Entra-authenticated effective scope
  applies before and after traversal and to entities, relationships, policy
  evidence, Gold rows, counts, diagnostics, prompts, caches, exports, and
  replay. Resource topology, security posture, ownership, principal
  identifiers, and incident context are sensitive operational metadata even
  when they are not classified as personal data.
- **Least privilege and credential handling:** use distinct managed workload
  identities and supported short-lived exchange where delegation is required.
  Browser bearer tokens, delegated tokens, authorization headers, and raw
  credential objects must not become backend authority or enter prompts,
  logs, telemetry, caches, checkpoints, or durable evidence.
- **Data minimization and privacy review:** retain only the bounded evidence
  needed to support and replay the answer. Any use of user/principal,
  customer, incident, or free-text data requires the existing enterprise
  classification, retention, residency, access-review, and deletion rules;
  this phase does not infer those rules.
- **Auditability and change governance:** retain a tamper-evident record of
  caller-safe principal, effective-scope fingerprint, question/seed,
  deterministic and native outcomes, source/query surface, policy and
  ontology/product generations, citations, freshness, disagreement,
  fallback reason, agent/version, timestamps, and canary decision. Native
  answers remain advisory and cannot bypass human approval or create a
  production change.
- **Risk-management baseline:** NIST AI RMF 1.0 and the voluntary NIST AI
  600-1 Generative AI Profile are appropriate governance references for
  documented human oversight, predeployment testing, ongoing measurement,
  incident response, and risk-based production sampling. They are guidance
  here, not a claim of statutory compliance.

independent reviewer. Rubric calibration requires SRE, network, security,

confirms evidence completeness. Production sampling should over-sample severe
incidents, high-degree graph seeds, truncated/partial results, stale or mixed

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Microsoft Agent Framework with the existing governed local orchestration and direct first-party Fabric adapters

**Version:** 1.15.0

**Rationale:** Phase 03.8 is a provider-adapter and grounding change, not a new orchestration problem. The existing MAF/Foundry specialist boundary and local deterministic governance remain authoritative.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Frameworkless direct Fabric integration | Retained only as a fallback if MAF cannot expose a required native surface; it must remain behind the same governed tool contracts. |
| LangGraph, CrewAI, or LlamaIndex | Would duplicate established orchestration, state, audit, and fallback controls without improving the native Fabric boundary. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — native Fabric IQ/Data Agent capabilities are Microsoft-specific, while model selection remains Foundry-managed and deterministic fallback remains local.

### 3.2 Minimal Foundry specialist entry point

This is the smallest standalone pattern for a pre-created, version-pinned
Foundry Prompt Agent. The Prompt Agent's stored model, system instructions,
and hosted tools are authoritative; client code cannot replace them.

```python
import asyncio
import os

from agent_framework.foundry import FoundryAgent
from azure.identity import ManagedIdentityCredential


async def main() -> None:
    client_id = os.getenv("AZURE_CLIENT_ID", "").strip()
    credential = (
        ManagedIdentityCredential(client_id=client_id)
        if client_id
        else ManagedIdentityCredential()
    )
    try:
        agent = FoundryAgent(
            project_endpoint=os.environ["FOUNDRY_PROJECT_ENDPOINT"],
            agent_name=os.environ["FOUNDRY_NETWORK_AGENT_NAME"],
            agent_version=os.environ["FOUNDRY_NETWORK_AGENT_VERSION"],
            credential=credential,
            timeout=20.0,
        )
        response = await asyncio.wait_for(
            agent.run(
                "Summarize only the supplied, cited topology evidence.",
                max_output_tokens=800,
            ),
            timeout=22.0,
        )
        print(response.text)
    finally:
        credential.close()


if __name__ == "__main__":
    asyncio.run(main())
```

In FastAPI or any existing event loop, call `await main()`-style coroutines;
do not call `asyncio.run()`. Production wiring belongs behind
`MicrosoftAgentFrameworkFoundryAgent` and the local
`OrchestrationService`, not in request handlers.

### 3.6 Framework- and service-specific pitfalls

1. **A Foundry Prompt Agent is service-configured.** `FoundryAgent` ignores
   client attempts to replace its model, stored instructions, or hosted tools;
   sampling and `response_format` are forwarded but can still be constrained
   by the stored agent/model. Pin agent name and version and validate the
   result locally.
2. **The Data Agent endpoint is MCP, not a one-shot REST method.** The official
   Fabric guidance requires `initialize`, `tools/list`, then `tools/call`.
   `gold_grounding_client.py` currently posts `tools/call` directly; it must
   not be treated as production-ready native grounding until the complete MCP
   flow and real ephemeral authorization header are implemented.
3. **Ontology binding is not proof of native consumption.** The current
   `_load_fabric_iq_adapter()` reads the OneLake ontology projection in
   process. It must continue to emit `onelake-ontology-projection`. Enable
   `source=fabric-iq` only when the documented first-party Ontology MCP
   endpoint or Foundry `fabric_iq_preview` tool actually answered. Microsoft
   now documents both native surfaces, including delegated OBO requirements;
   the deployed payload shape, relationship/path identifiers, citations,
   freshness, generation metadata, and independent tool telemetry remain
   blocking live checkpoints and must not be guessed.
4. **Streaming is not the validation boundary.** Structured output becomes
   trustworthy only after the final response is assembled and Pydantic
   validation succeeds. In addition, Agent Framework issue #7418 reports a
   lost structured `value` on a streaming harness/tool-approval path. Use
   non-streaming `await` for governed specialist findings in this phase.
5. **Framework state is not a secret store.** Open issues document checkpoint
   key-collision data loss (#8256) and compaction/middleware reconciliation
   surprises (#8313). Keep canonical thread/evidence state in the existing
   local stores with string keys and explicit schemas; never place access
   tokens or authorization headers in an `AgentSession`, checkpoint, prompt,
   trace, exception, cache, or ledger.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### 4.1 Authority-preserving execution order

Implement one orchestration path; do not create a second agent-owned control
plane.

1. Authenticate the API caller and derive **effective scope** through the
   existing RBAC path.
2. Resolve every seed resource through `InventoryReadPort`. Reject an unknown
   or out-of-scope seed before any Fabric or Foundry call. Inventory remains
   the sole identity/lifecycle authority.
3. Run deterministic checks first and retain their findings regardless of
   native availability.
4. If the default-off rollout gate permits native work, launch the allowlisted
   Fabric IQ traversal and optional Gold Data Agent query concurrently, each
   with an independent timeout and cancellation path.
5. Validate provider payloads into `NativeResult`, re-check every returned
   entity/resource against effective scope and inventory, and close every
   citation. Native evidence that conflicts with inventory is marked
   conflicting and cannot replace inventory fields.
6. If native Fabric IQ is disabled, denied, empty, timed out, malformed,
   stale, or unavailable, call the existing OneLake projection under its own
   `onelake-ontology-projection` provenance. Native and projection results
   must never be merged under one source label.
7. Give the version-pinned Foundry specialist only bounded, validated,
   token-free evidence. Validate the structured specialist response. If
   Foundry fails, return the deterministic answer plus accepted native/fallback
   evidence without model narration.
8. Record the token-free decision, source outcomes, provenance, generation,
   citations, disagreement, and fallback reason in the existing ledger.

The native Fabric IQ client must be an injected port until Microsoft publishes
or enables a supported query API for this tenant. The adapter may use only
that first-party surface. `TopologyOntologyBinder` remains the publication and
binding step; it is not itself a traversal client.

Use the existing specialist manifest contract:

| Setting | Required value |
|---|---|
| Specialist model | `gpt-4.1`, stored in each Foundry Prompt Agent |
| Agent definition | Pin `FOUNDRY_<SPECIALIST>_AGENT_VERSION`; current manifest version is `1` |

| End-to-end enrichment | 30-second hard deadline; cancellation immediately selects deterministic/projection fallback |

definition must be tested for the schema and token limit before canary.
Malformed output is a failed optional enrichment, not permission to parse
best-effort prose.

### 4.3 Core async pattern

The implementation should follow this shape. Names ending in `Port` are local
interfaces, not invented Microsoft SDK methods:

```python
from __future__ import annotations

import asyncio
from collections.abc import Awaitable, Callable
from typing import Any, Protocol

from pydantic import ValidationError


class NativeFabricIQPort(Protocol):
    async def query(
        self, *, operation: str, subject_id: str, ontology_id: str
    ) -> dict[str, Any]: ...


async def _bounded(
    call: Callable[[], Awaitable[dict[str, Any]]], *, timeout: float
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    return await asyncio.wait_for(call(), timeout=timeout)


async def enrich_turn(
    *,
    subject_id: str,
    operation: str,
    effective_scope: Any,
    inventory: Any,
    native_iq: NativeFabricIQPort | None,
    query_gold: Callable[[], Awaitable[dict[str, Any]]] | None,
    query_projection: Callable[[], Awaitable[dict[str, Any]]],
    ontology_id: str,
) -> tuple[Any, Any]:
    # Identity/scope is established locally before optional provider calls.
    canonical = inventory.get_resource(effective_scope, subject_id)
    if canonical is None:
        raise PermissionError("subject is unknown or outside effective scope")

    native_task = (
        asyncio.create_task(
            _bounded(
                lambda: native_iq.query(
                    operation=operation,
                    subject_id=subject_id,
                    ontology_id=ontology_id,
                ),
                timeout=8.0,
            )
        )
        if native_iq is not None
        else None
    )
    gold_task = (
        asyncio.create_task(_bounded(query_gold, timeout=20.0))
        if query_gold is not None
        else None
    )

    async def accepted(task: asyncio.Task[dict[str, Any]] | None) -> Any | None:
        if task is None:
            return None
        try:
            raw = await task
            result = NativeResult.model_validate(raw)  # defined in §4b.1
            validate_result_scope(result, effective_scope, inventory)
            return result
        except (TimeoutError, ValidationError, PermissionError, RuntimeError):
            return None

    native_result, gold_result = await asyncio.gather(
        accepted(native_task), accepted(gold_task)
    )
    if native_result is None:
        # This source is explicitly the local projection, never Fabric IQ.
        projection_raw = await _bounded(query_projection, timeout=5.0)
        native_result = NativeResult.model_validate(projection_raw)
        validate_result_scope(native_result, effective_scope, inventory)

    return native_result, gold_result
```

The concrete Fabric IQ implementation must fill `NativeResult` with the
actual ontology ID, ontology/product generation, query surface, observed time,
and stable entity/relationship references returned by the supported service.
Do not route the native port back to `runtime.service.ontology_query`.

| `fabric_iq_topology` | Network specialist through local orchestrator | One inventory-resolved subject; max 2 hops | At most 50 entities/100 relationships; all in effective scope; stable relationship citations; `source=fabric-iq` only for a native answer |
| `fabric_iq_relationships` | Network/governance specialists | One inventory-resolved subject and allowlisted relationship types | Direction and relationship type preserved; ontology generation recorded |

No Phase 03.8 flow uses an Azure inspection tool wrapper for this native
traversal or grounding work. Azure resource inspection uses standard `az`;
Fabric/Foundry data-plane gaps use official REST or locked first-party SDKs.

Create one managed-identity credential per process/provider and let the Azure
Identity library cache and renew access tokens. For Fabric, acquire
`https://api.fabric.microsoft.com/.default` immediately before opening the MCP
request, attach it only as an in-memory `Authorization: Bearer ...` header, and
discard the header after the call. Do not put `fabric_delegated_token` in
`GoldGroundingScope` or any Pydantic/durable model. For user-context access,
the backend must exchange the authenticated user assertion through a
first-party supported flow and then apply the same local effective scope; it
must never forward or trust a browser-supplied Fabric bearer token.

The Data Agent adapter must implement the documented MCP lifecycle:
`initialize` -> initialized notification -> `tools/list` -> discover the one
tool and its question argument -> `tools/call`. Preserve the negotiated MCP
session header in memory only. If the server returns an MCP task, poll
`tasks/get` under the same deadline and send `tasks/cancel` on local
cancellation. `httpx.AsyncClient` can implement this protocol with the
existing dependency, so no dependency change is justified in this phase.

### 4.5 State, provenance, and fallback

- **Durable state:** retain only effective-scope fingerprint, caller-safe
  principal identifier, seed IDs, agent name/version, ontology and data
  generations, status, citations, hashes, timing, and fallback decision.
  Never retain credentials or complete authorization headers.
- **Conversation state:** the local thread/evidence ledger remains
  authoritative. If an `AgentSession` is used, retain only its opaque ID,
  bind it to the authorized principal and effective scope, and reauthorize on
  every resume.
- **Provenance names:** `fabric-iq` means an actual native Fabric IQ query;
  `onelake-ontology-projection` means the current in-process Delta projection;
  `fabric-data-agent-gold` means the published Data Agent; deterministic
  engines retain their existing exact source names.
- **Fallback order:** deterministic baseline -> native Fabric IQ enrichment
  when allowed -> separately labeled OneLake projection when native is not
  accepted -> deterministic response if Foundry narration is not accepted.
  Empty, denied, unavailable, stale, malformed, and timed-out are distinct
  outcomes.
- **Rollout:** default-off -> shadow (never displayed) -> bounded canary ->
  explicit enablement. The kill switch stops new native/Data Agent calls and
  immediately returns to deterministic plus OneLake projection without
  deleting evidence or Fabric artifacts.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### 4b.1 Structured outputs with Pydantic

Use one strict provider envelope before adapting to `ToolResult`. It contains
no credential field by design:

```python
from datetime import datetime
from typing import Literal

from pydantic import (
    BaseModel,
    ConfigDict,
    Field,
    field_validator,
    model_validator,
)


class StrictModel(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(
        extra="forbid",
        strict=True,
        str_strip_whitespace=True,
    )


class NativeCitation(StrictModel):
    citation_id: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=200)
    source_ref: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=512)
    entity_id: str = Field(default="", max_length=512)
    relationship_id: str = Field(default="", max_length=256)
    observed_at: datetime
    confidence: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0)

    @field_validator("observed_at")
    @classmethod
    def require_timezone(cls, value: datetime) -> datetime:
        if value.tzinfo is None or value.utcoffset() is None:
            raise ValueError("observed_at must include a timezone")
        return value


class NativeFact(StrictModel):
    fact_id: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=160)
    subject_id: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=512)
    predicate: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=128)
    object_id: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=512)
    summary: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=240)
    citation_ids: list[str] = Field(min_length=1, max_length=8)


class NativeResult(StrictModel):
    schema_version: Literal["fabric-native-result.v1"]
    source: Literal[
        "fabric-iq",
        "fabric-data-agent-gold",
        "onelake-ontology-projection",
    ]
    operation: Literal[
        "relationships", "topology", "policy", "gold_grounding"
    ]
    status: Literal[
        "ok", "empty", "partial", "unavailable", "denied", "stale"
    ]
    query_surface: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=128)
    ontology_id: str = Field(default="", max_length=128)
    ontology_generation: str = Field(default="", max_length=128)
    product_generation: str = Field(default="", max_length=128)
    authorization_mode: Literal["managed-identity", "delegated-exchange"]
    scope_fingerprint: str = Field(min_length=16, max_length=128)
    scope_checked: Literal[True]
    identity_authority: Literal[False] = False
    observed_at: datetime
    facts: list[NativeFact] = Field(default_factory=list, max_length=100)
    citations: list[NativeCitation] = Field(
        default_factory=list, max_length=100
    )
    warnings: list[str] = Field(default_factory=list, max_length=16)

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def close_citations_and_status(self) -> "NativeResult":
        citation_ids = [item.citation_id for item in self.citations]
        if len(citation_ids) != len(set(citation_ids)):
            raise ValueError("citation IDs must be unique")
        known = set(citation_ids)
        if any(not set(fact.citation_ids) <= known for fact in self.facts):
            raise ValueError("every fact citation must close locally")
        if self.status == "ok" and not self.facts:
            raise ValueError("ok requires at least one fact")
        if self.status in {"empty", "unavailable", "denied"} and self.facts:
            raise ValueError(f"{self.status} must not contain accepted facts")
        if self.source == "fabric-iq" and not self.ontology_id:
            raise ValueError("native Fabric IQ requires ontology_id")
        if (
            self.source == "onelake-ontology-projection"
            and self.query_surface != "in-process-onelake-projection"
        ):
            raise ValueError("projection provenance is inconsistent")
        return self
```

Pydantic cannot determine caller authorization by itself. Immediately after
`model_validate`, perform a semantic validator that resolves every
`subject_id`, `object_id`, and citation entity through `InventoryReadPort`
under the already-derived effective scope, compares
`scope_fingerprint`, enforces allowed relationship types/hop counts, and
rejects inventory identity/lifecycle conflicts.

For compatible Agent Framework agents, request native structured output with:

```python
async def run_structured(agent, prompt: str) -> NativeResult:
    response = await agent.run(
        prompt,
        max_output_tokens=800,
        options={"response_format": NativeResult, "temperature": 0},
    )
    return (
        response.value
        if isinstance(response.value, NativeResult)
        else NativeResult.model_validate_json(response.text)
    )
```

Because `FoundryAgent` only forwards `response_format` and the stored agent or
model can constrain it, local validation is mandatory. Retry model schema
failures at most **two** times with the same evidence, a compact list of
Pydantic error paths, and no raw secret/provider payload. Never ask a second
model to “repair” native service evidence. Log correlation ID, source,
agent/version, schema version, attempt, error path/type, response byte count
and hash, latency, and fallback reason—not raw tokens or authorization
headers. After two failed corrections, surface validated deterministic
fallback with `fallback_reason=structured_output_invalid`; do not expose
best-effort JSON as evidence. A native adapter schema failure receives at most
one transient re-fetch if still within its deadline, then falls back without
LLM repair.

### 4b.2 Async-first design

- MAF `agent.run(...)` is awaitable; `agent.run(..., stream=True)` returns an
  async stream. Fabric IQ and Data Agent adapters must use
  `httpx.AsyncClient`, not blocking `httpx.post`, `time.sleep`, or sync token
  acquisition on the event-loop thread. If using the synchronous Azure
  Identity credential shared with `FoundryAgent`, acquire a direct Fabric
  token with `await asyncio.to_thread(credential.get_token, FABRIC_SCOPE)`.
- Use `asyncio.wait_for`/`asyncio.timeout` for each dependency and one outer
  turn deadline. Cancel provider work when the caller disconnects or the
  orchestration budget expires; cancellation is not a retryable failure.
- Never call `asyncio.run()` from FastAPI, notebooks, or another running event
  loop. The standalone `__main__` example is the only appropriate use.
- Stream prose only when early UX is more important than a typed contract.
  For specialist evidence, `await` the non-streaming final response and
  validate it. If streaming is later enabled, consume the stream and call
  `get_final_response()` before reading `value`; never validate partial
  chunks.

### 4b.3 Prompt engineering discipline

- Store the specialist's immutable role, safety rules, output schema intent,
  and “deterministic/inventory authority” rules as **system instructions in
  the versioned Foundry Prompt Agent**. `FoundryAgent` treats those stored
  instructions as authoritative.
- Send the operator question and the bounded, already-authorized evidence as
  user content. Delimit and label evidence as untrusted data. Do not place
  policy overrides, credentials, headers, internal chain-of-thought, or
  unvalidated native text in the user prompt.
- Keep 2–4 canonical few-shot examples in the versioned agent definition.
  Retrieve additional examples dynamically only from a trusted,
  scope-independent local example set, selected by operation and schema
  version—not from user content or native provider prose.
- Set `max_output_tokens=800` on every specialist invocation. The stored
  model's temperature is `0`. Reject answers that introduce uncited resources,
  mutation instructions, unsupported authority, or fields outside the schema.

### 4b.4 Context-window management

- Run graph traversal from inventory-resolved seeds only; default to two hops,
  50 entities, 100 relationships, and allowlisted predicates. Rank shortest
  paths and operationally critical dependencies first. Include stable IDs,
  direction, generation, freshness, and citations rather than raw graph rows.
- Rank Gold citations by scope validity, confidence, and newest `as_of`, then
  truncate. Preserve at least one citation per retained fact; drop the fact if
  its citation cannot fit. Keep the existing 24,000-character whole-turn
  ceiling and 6,000-character Gold budget until token-based accounting
  replaces them.
- For conversations and multi-specialist synthesis, retain the existing
  citation-only history and summarize accepted findings into IDs, status,
  source, observation state, and uncertainty. Rehydrate current evidence from
  local governed stores rather than replaying raw prior provider payloads.
- Do not rely on MAF compaction for authority or evidence retention. If later
  enabled, test middleware order and summary reconciliation against #8313 and
  ensure every summary preserves citation and source distinctions. Tokens
  remain excluded before and after compaction.

### 4b.5 Cost and latency budget

Budget a normal turn for at most one `gpt-4.1` specialist call with
approximately 6,000 input and 800 output tokens. Using planning rates
`$2/M` input tokens and `$8/M` output tokens, the estimate is:

```text
(6,000 × $2 + 800 × $8) / 1,000,000 = $0.0184 per specialist turn
10,000 such turns/month = $184/month
```

This is a budgeting assumption, not an Azure quote; replace both rates with
the deployed Foundry region's current prices before canary. Two structured
output retries create a worst-case model multiplier of 3, so track first-pass
schema success and alert before retries normalize excess spend. Fabric IQ and
Data Agent usage also consumes Fabric capacity; report capacity/chargeback
separately rather than pretending it is included in token cost.

Use an exact-match cache only for validated read-only results, keyed by
`tenant + effective_scope_fingerprint + operation + normalized seed IDs +
ontology/product generation + data-agent version`. Never cache tokens,
authorization headers, denied results across principals, or entries beyond
their freshness. Semantic caching is allowed only after an evaluation proves
scope isolation and citation equivalence; otherwise disable it. Use cheaper
models only for non-authoritative classification, routing, or summarization;
the deterministic keyword router should remain the zero-model fallback.

Target latency is deterministic baseline <=2 seconds, native Fabric IQ <=12
seconds, Data Agent <=20 seconds, Foundry specialist <=22 seconds, and the
parallelized end-to-end enrichment path <=30 seconds. Retry only 429, 500,
502, 503, 504, connection reset, or connect/read timeout with exponential
backoff plus jitter and only while the outer deadline has room. Do not retry
400/401/403, scope denial, a valid empty result, stale evidence, semantic
conflict, or caller cancellation. Open the existing circuit breaker after
repeated dependency failure and immediately select the deterministic,
separately labeled fallback.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### 5.1 Decision rule and four-arm experiment

Phase 03.8 is successful only if native enrichment improves a reviewed
operator answer while every deterministic authority and safety property is
non-inferior. A quality lift cannot compensate for one scope, identity,
citation, policy, fallback, credential, or audit failure.

Run every reference case as a paired four-arm experiment:

| Arm | Enabled evidence path | Required source labels |
|---|---|---|
| A — deterministic | Inventory and deterministic checks only | Existing deterministic source names |
| B — OneLake baseline | Arm A plus the current in-process ontology projection | `onelake-ontology-projection` |
| C — native Fabric IQ | Arm A plus native topology/relationship/policy context; use projection only when the injected case requires fallback | `fabric-iq`, or separately labeled fallback |
| D — native + Data Agent | Arm C plus curated Gold grounding through the published Data Agent | `fabric-iq` and `fabric-data-agent-gold`, or separately labeled fallbacks |

The harness must pin the same question, caller-safe principal, effective
scope, inventory snapshot, deterministic findings, policy/check revisions,
ontology/product/Gold generation, failure injection, budgets, prompt-agent
version, and reference truth for all four arms. Native, projection, and Gold
fixtures must be content-equivalent views of that frozen generation; a run
with generation drift is invalid, not an experimental result. Arm assignment
is hidden from answer reviewers, output order is randomized, model settings
are fixed, and all paired results retain the case ID and input/evidence
digests. Report every arm, not only the winning arm.

The existing `groundedness_mean=0.283` in
`.planning/eval/gold-grounding-baseline.json` remains a useful
data-availability baseline, but `scripts/evaluate_agents.py` explicitly does
not treat it as answer quality. It must not be cited as the Phase 03.8 quality
lift. Extend that harness to emit the four-arm, generation-pinned evidence
below while preserving its existing fail-closed artifact validation.

### 5.4 Judge calibration and statistical interpretation

The answer judge sees only the frozen question, reference facts, accepted
evidence, and answer; it does not see the arm. Two humans independently score
a balanced calibration sample of at least 40 outputs across all arms,
families, and failure states. Use the judge only after Spearman correlation
with adjudicated human scores is >=0.80 and quadratic weighted kappa is
>=0.70, with no false pass on a critical case. Recalibrate on every
judge/prompt/rubric change and quarterly. If calibration fails, humans score
all answer-quality outputs; code gates are never replaced by the judge.

Predeclare the contrasts in §5.2 before opening results. Use case-paired BCa
bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples), report family-level results only when
`n>=20`, and report the CI even when the mean passes. A confidence interval
crossing zero is “no demonstrated lift,” not a pass. Human reviewers inspect
all critical code failures, every arm disagreement on a high-severity case,
and at least 10% of otherwise passing outputs.

| Signal | Sampling and disposition |
|---|---|
| Native versus deterministic/OneLake disagreement | Score all disagreements by entity/edge/policy field and generation. Domain owners adjudicate high-severity or authority conflicts; add confirmed new patterns to the next reference-set version. |
| Citation/provenance/freshness replay | Replay 100% of rejected results and a risk-weighted sample of accepted citations. Send broken rows/edges or generation drift to the Fabric steward; disable the affected publication when validity is not 100%. |
| Traversal and policy drift | Re-run the frozen canonical graph and policy fixtures nightly and after ontology, binding, policy, Data Agent, or prompt-agent publication. Hold the changed arm on any exactness regression. |
| Answer-quality lift | Score at least 100 sampled eligible turns/week when volume permits, oversampling high-degree, partial, stale, conflict, fallback, and severe-incident cases. Recompute paired/calibrated quality by operation and source; no aggregate score may hide a failing stratum. |
| Latency, reliability, and cost drift | Compare p50/p95/p99, retry, timeout, circuit-open, tokens, dollars, and Fabric capacity with the approved shadow baseline by arm and operation. Tune budgets or disable optional calls rather than weakening fallback. |
| Emerging failures and reviewer disagreement | Cluster bounded failure reason codes and reviewer/judge disagreement without sending data to third parties. Human-review novel or rising clusters and create a minimally sufficient regression case. |

### 6.3 Default-off promotion and rollback

| Stage | Exposure | Entry/exit gate |
|---|---|---|
| Default off | Zero native/Data Agent calls; A+B behavior remains available | Remain here until deterministic tests, the 88-case four-arm run, trace redaction, cost accounting, and kill-switch exercise pass. |
| Shadow | At most 5% of explicitly allowlisted topology/policy turns call C/D; no native result is displayed | Close the deploy/start plan after writing `rollout/shadow-01/start.json`; wait outside an active executor; then validate >=200 paired turns and >=7 elapsed days into the distinct immutable `rollout/shadow-01/final.json`. Require all Critical gates, quality CIs, complete audit, and named human approvals. |
| Canary 1 | Display C/D to <=1% of eligible requests and named services/principals | Close the deploy/start plan after writing `rollout/canary1-01/start.json`; wait externally; validate >=200 displayed turns and >=7 elapsed days into `rollout/canary1-01/final.json`; then require a signed Canary 1 exit review. |
| Canary 2 | Display C/D to <=5% of the same bounded use cases | A distinct signed Canary 2 authorization must exist before deployment. Close the deploy/start plan, wait externally, then validate >=500 additional turns and >=14 elapsed days into `rollout/canary2-01/final.json` with a repeated four-arm run. |
| Final state in this plan set | Native/Data Agent calls killed/off after rollback drill | Produce a signed `HOLD_OFF` final decision. No direct enabled promotion is authorized after kill/rollback. |

Activate the kill switch immediately for any actual scope/credential leak,
identity-authority override, displayed invalid citation/provenance, stale fact
presented as current, deterministic policy reversal, mutation authorization,
missing deterministic fallback, or missing durable audit record. Also roll
the affected arm back when end-to-end p95 exceeds 30 seconds, fallback exceeds
20%, unqualified native/deterministic disagreement is nonzero, total
disagreement exceeds 10%, or incremental p95 cost exceeds $0.025 for two
consecutive 15-minute windows; roll back immediately when daily incremental
spend exceeds $25 or the absolute per-turn ceiling exceeds $0.0552. Rollback
stops new native/Data Agent calls, preserves all evidence, and returns to
deterministic plus separately labeled OneLake projection. This plan set then
ends in signed HOLD/off. Any later enabled decision requires a new versioned
shadow re-entry authorization, a new elapsed evidence interval, human approval,
and replanning; no rollback, canary, or shadow report is rewritten.

## 7. Production Monitoring

### 7.1 Tracing and telemetry contract

Use the existing OpenTelemetry -> Application Insights path in production.
Phoenix remains the local/offline viewer described in §5.5; do not install the
conflicting `eval` extra in the API image and do not export sensitive
production content to a third party.

Create one turn span with child spans for scope derivation, inventory seed
resolution, deterministic checks, native Fabric IQ, Data Agent MCP lifecycle,
OneLake projection, Foundry narration, schema/scope/citation/freshness/policy
validation, fallback selection, display validation, and ledger write. Required
token-free attributes are:

`operation`, `rollout_stage`, `arm`, `source`, `provider_outcome`,
`query_surface`, `authorization_mode`, `scope_fingerprint_hash`,
`ontology_generation_hash`, `product_generation_hash`, `freshness_class`,
`citation_count`, `result_count_bucket`, `hop_count`, `retry_count`,
`fallback_reason`, `disagreement_class`, `agent_version`, `schema_version`,
`duration_ms`, `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `estimated_cost_usd`,
`fabric_capacity_cost`, `validation_decision`, and `kill_switch_state`.

Never attach questions, raw prompts/provider payloads, resource/principal
names, row contents, tokens, headers, credential objects, or unbounded error
text to metric dimensions. Keep case/correlation IDs only in access-controlled
traces and ledgers, not as metric labels.

### 7.2 Metrics and alert thresholds

Extend `GoldGroundingMetrics` rather than creating a parallel telemetry
system. Use bounded dimensions `source`, `operation`, `outcome`,
`rollout_stage`, and enumerated `fallback_reason`.

| Metric | Production calculation | Alert / action |
|---|---|---|
| Native-call rate | `fabric_native.calls / eligible_turns`, separately for Fabric IQ and Data Agent | **Critical:** any call while default-off/kill-switch-on or rate above configured stage by >1 percentage point. Investigate zero calls for 30 minutes while an enabled canary has eligible traffic. |
| Provider outcomes | Count/rate of `ok`, `empty`, `partial`, `denied`, `stale`, `timeout`, `malformed`, `conflicting`, `unavailable` by source/operation | Warn when non-`ok` exceeds shadow baseline by 5 points for 30 minutes; rollback affected arm when unavailable+timeout >20% for two 15-minute windows. Denied/cross-scope remains a security-review signal, never a success conversion. |
| Latency and reliability | Dependency and end-to-end p50/p95/p99, retries, cancellation, timeout, circuit-open, completed deterministic response | Page/rollback on two 15-minute windows above Fabric IQ 12 s, Data Agent 20 s, Foundry 22 s, or end-to-end 30 s p95. Critical if optional failure yields no deterministic response. |
| Fallback | Fallback count/rate by exact reason and whether deterministic payload survived | Critical on any incorrect/missing fallback or source mislabel. Warn at >10%; rollback at >20% for two 15-minute windows, excluding deliberate fault drills and default-off traffic. |
| Citation and provenance | Material claims, citations, closure/replay success, source-label accuracy, generation consistency | Critical/page and rollback on validity, coverage, source accuracy, or accepted generation consistency below 100%. Empty/degraded fabricated-citation count must remain zero. |
| Freshness | Evidence age by source, freshness class, stale rejection, accepted stale/mixed-age count | Critical and rollback on accepted stale/mixed-generation count >0. Warn when stale/rejected rate rises >5 points above shadow baseline for 30 minutes. |
| Violations blocked | Scope, secret, schema, hop/result, policy-authority, identity-conflict, and mutation blocks | Page security immediately for suspected scope/secret disclosure; investigate every block with the ledger. A blocked result is evidence the guardrail worked, but any post-display violation is a kill-switch event. |
| Cost and usage | Input/output tokens, model estimate, Fabric capacity/chargeback, per-arm incremental p50/p95/max and daily total | Warn at 80% of any §5.2 limit. Roll back under §6.3 at p95 >$0.025 twice, any turn >$0.0552, or daily >$25. Missing/NaN/negative accounting is a failed canary measurement. |
| Native/deterministic disagreement | Exact entity, directed-edge, affected-set, policy, identity, freshness, and citation disagreement rate; qualified versus unqualified | Review every high-severity/authority conflict. Warn at `max(5%, shadow baseline +3 points)` for 1 hour; rollback at >10% twice or any unqualified displayed disagreement. |
| Drift | Population Stability Index over operation, predicate, hops, result-size bucket, freshness, outcomes, fallback reasons, and answer-quality strata | Warn at PSI >0.20 versus approved shadow baseline or when weekly paired quality mean drops >=0.25 points; hold promotion when a quality-lift CI crosses zero. Rebuild labels rather than silently retuning thresholds. |
| Audit completeness | Eligible turns with complete required spans plus durable ledger decision/source/generation/citation/fallback/usage fields | Critical and rollback on any missing durable record in shadow/canary. Alert at trace-field completeness <99.9%; telemetry loss never deletes the ledger requirement. |

Alerts must link to sanitized runbooks and correlation IDs, not raw evidence.
Every alert records owner, disposition, kill-switch decision, and reference-set
follow-up.

### 7.3 Sampling and review cadence

- Trace and retain evaluation metadata for 100% of native calls in shadow,
  both canary stages, and the first 14 days after explicit enablement.
- Always retain all denied, blocked, stale, partial, timeout, malformed,
  conflicting, unavailable, fallback, disagreement, invalid-citation,
  schema-failure, slow, high-cost, and kill-switch events. Guardrail checks and
  durable ledger writes are never sampled.
- After 14 stable days, sample at least 10% of ordinary successful native
  traces while retaining 100% of the risk events above. Keep low-cardinality
  counters unsampled.
- Send at least 100 eligible turns/week through offline answer-quality scoring
  when volume permits and human-review at least 20 risk-weighted turns/week,
  including every severe incident and authority disagreement.
- Review operational dashboards daily during shadow/canary, quality and drift
  weekly, judge calibration quarterly, and thresholds/reference data after
  every ontology, policy, Data Agent, prompt-agent, model, or material adapter
  change.
