  <read_first>

  <decision>Choose the D-02/D-04 inventory SQL physical-generation strategy and namespaced product-pointer layout before any schema or pointer artifact is created.</decision>
  <context>The decision is rated costly: later reversal requires coordinated schema/backfill/reader/retention changes, while a pointer not bound into SQL cannot provide snapshot isolation or atomic rollback. It must not alter the existing ontology `current.json` path or Exposure Gold pointer.</context>
  <options>
    <option id="generation-shadow-namespaced-pointer" recommended="true">
      <name>Generation-qualified shadow tables plus namespaced CAS product pointer</name>
      <pros>Matches RESEARCH.md’s recommendation; supports same-generation shadow comparison, one-pointer rollback, retained old tables, and no mutation of current ontology/Exposure pointer contracts.</pros>
      <cons>Temporarily duplicates physical inventory writes and increases retained storage until the operational retirement gate.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="evolve-existing-tables-in-place">
      <name>Add product-generation columns to existing inventory tables in place</name>
      <pros>Uses fewer physical table names and may reduce temporary storage duplication.</pros>
      <cons>Requires coordinated backfill and makes old/new reader isolation and rollback harder during migration.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="compatibility-view-or-alias">
      <name>Use a Fabric-supported compatibility view/alias over generation-qualified storage</name>
      <pros>Can preserve stable logical names for named consumers while physical generations remain immutable.</pros>
      <cons>Requires live proof that the selected Fabric alias/view mechanism preserves metadata visibility, permissions, generation predicates, and rollback behavior.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <reversibility rating="costly">Changing the selected physical schema or pointer namespace after cutover requires retained-data compatibility and coordinated reader migration.</reversibility>
