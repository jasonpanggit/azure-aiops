Add the generalized `ResourceObservation` contract and the family registry, alongside the existing models rather than replacing them.

Purpose: INV-08, and the contract Phases 8 and 9 depend on. Per D-08 the decisive property is **lossless passthrough of the provider `properties` payload**. A flattened string map cannot express that, and locking one here would make the Phase 8 pivot impossible. Per D-09 typed fields are derived projections alongside the payload. Per D-11/D-12 the contract is bounded to the resource identity plane.

Output: `ResourceObservation`, `MachineObservation` re-expressed as a specialization with an unchanged serialized shape, a family registry, and a losslessness proof.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 1: Decide the lossless properties payload encoding</name>
  <decision>How the lossless provider `properties` payload is carried on `ResourceObservation` and serialized into the published dataset row.</decision>
  <context>
D-08 locks *that* the payload is carried losslessly — that is settled and not being reopened. What is the agent's discretion, and what this checkpoint decides, is *how*: the in-memory field type and the on-dataset serialization.

This is a one-way decision. Phase 8's entire premise is that Hermes can stop querying ARG for resource identity and read everything it reads today from inventory instead. Hermes reads `properties` by descending several levels and re-storing raw sub-bags wholesale. Whatever encoding is chosen here becomes the shape every downstream normalizer reads, and changing it later means re-cutting the contract after consumers depend on it.

The published rows are written as JSONL and as Parquet in the same generation. Nested payloads and Parquet columnar typing interact — an approach that is clean in JSONL may be lossy or unstable in Parquet when payload shapes vary across resource types.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="native-nested">
      <name>Carry the payload as a native nested mapping and let the writers serialize it structurally</name>
      <pros>Most faithful to the provider shape; consumers read it exactly as they read the ARG payload today with no decode step</pros>
      <cons>Parquet schema inference across heterogeneous resource types is the risk; ragged payloads across families may produce unstable or failing column types</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="encoded-blob">
      <name>Carry the payload as a native nested mapping in memory but serialize it to a single encoded text column in the published datasets</name>
      <pros>Immune to Parquet schema instability across heterogeneous types; guarantees byte-level round-trip; predictable dataset shape per family</pros>
      <cons>Adds a decode step for every consumer; the payload is opaque to any query engine reading the dataset directly</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select: native-nested, encoded-blob, or describe an alternative</resume-signal>
  <reversibility rating="one-way">The chosen encoding becomes the contract every Phase 8 and Phase 9 consumer reads; changing it after consumers depend on it requires re-cutting the contract.</reversibility>
</task>
